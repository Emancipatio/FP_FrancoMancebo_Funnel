# Playbooks for Part 2 · First hours back today

Build every piece in full. Fill names, senders, meeting titles and tools from what you saw in Part 1. Write templates in the founder's voice (ask for two lines of their own writing if you have none).

## Inbox

1. **Triage rules (one page).** Four buckets with examples from their inbox:
   - Answer alone: questions with a known answer (pricing, links, availability, status).
   - Draft for me: anything to a client or partner that needs judgement.
   - Archive: newsletters, notifications, receipts (after saving them).
   - Reaches me now: named people, money over their decision limit, legal, anything urgent.
2. **5 Gmail filter searches** to paste into Gmail search → "Create filter". Real syntax, built from their senders, for example `from:(@stripe.com OR @quickbooks.com) subject:(receipt OR invoice)` → label "Money", skip inbox.
3. **5 reply templates** for the most common replies in their sent mail, each with a subject line and a body under 80 words.
4. **Two inbox times a day** (suggest times) and one line to add to their signature if they want to set expectations.

## Scheduling

1. **Calendar rules:** meeting hours, buffers, max meetings per day, a no-meeting block, what gets a yes without them, what needs them.
2. **Booking blocks:** 2–3 weekly blocks to offer instead of back-and-forth, with exact days and times.
3. **3 scheduling replies:** offering times, rescheduling, and declining politely, each under 60 words.
4. If they use a booking tool, the exact settings to change in it. If not, suggest using their calendar's own booking page (don't name paid tools).

## Meetings

1. A table of their recurring and internal meetings: name · length · attendees · keep / shorten / make async / send someone else, with one-line reasons.
2. **An async update template** to replace one status meeting (done / in progress / blocked / needs a decision).
3. **2 polite messages:** one to shorten a meeting, one to step out of a meeting someone else can run.
4. A one-page **meeting brief template** for the meetings they keep.

## Follow-ups

1. A **follow-up tracker** layout (who · what · promised by · next nudge date · status) they can paste into a sheet.
2. **3 follow-up templates:** gentle nudge (day 3), firm nudge (day 7), close the loop (day 14).
3. A rule: what gets followed up without them and what they want to see first.

## Admin and money

1. A **monthly money checklist** an assistant can run: invoices out, receipts saved, bills paid (with their approval for payments), overdue chasing, month-end summary.
2. **2 templates:** an overdue invoice reminder and a receipt request.
3. Approval rule: payments always need the founder's yes; everything else follows their decision limit.
